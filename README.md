## Thesis in one paragraph

NioCorp is a **high-risk, development-stage critical-minerals bet** whose outcome no longer turns mainly on geology — the deposit is defined — but on **six variables**: (1) **closing project financing**, anchored by an in-review EXIM debt application of up to ~$800M; (2) whether **binding scandium demand** materializes, since scandium drives ~62% of project revenue but the global market is only ~30–40 t/yr against Elk Creek's planned ~100 t/yr; (3) whether **government support becomes binding** — an offtake, price floor, or stockpile purchase that names NioCorp — rather than political language; (4) whether the **pending updated feasibility study** confirms survivable economics versus the aging 2022 study; (5) **dilution risk**, since the company funds itself by serial equity issuance into a ~$1.1B capex gap; and (6) **construction execution** on budget and schedule. Get those right and the published economics imply large upside; miss on financing or scandium demand and the equity faces severe dilution or worse.

## Blunt thesis stack (what has to be true, in order of importance)

1. **Full financing closes** — EXIM debt (~$800M) or equivalent project debt becomes binding and the ~$1.1B capital stack is funded. Everything else is secondary to this.
2. **Binding scandium demand appears** — an offtake, stockpile, or price floor absorbs a meaningful share of the ~85 t/yr of currently-uncommitted scandium. This is the harder of the two core problems.
3. **Government support becomes binding, not just political** — committed dollars/contracts naming NioCorp, versus EOs and praise that any competitor also receives.
4. **Updated feasibility study confirms economics** — capex at/below ~$1.1–1.2B with realistic post-2019 costs, validated recoveries, and rare-earth upside, not a downgrade.
5. **Dilution remains survivable** — the equity needed to fill the gap is raised at prices that don't gut existing holders (worst case modeled near a tripling of the share count).
6. **Construction starts and stays on budget** — the $44.6M portal and full build proceed without material overrun or delay.
7. **Product qualification converts into revenue** — buyers technically qualify Elk Creek material and offtakes turn into cash, not term sheets.

## Bull case

- Elk Creek is a **large, defined, strategically located U.S. deposit** of minerals on the federal critical-minerals list (scandium, niobium, titanium, plus rare-earth potential).
- The product slate **fits current critical-minerals policy** squarely (domestic supply, China-dependence reduction, defense industrial base).
- **DoD has already funded scandium work** ($10M DPA Title III; Lockheed Martin Skunk Works alloy development) — a foot in the door and technology validation.
- The **EXIM path could solve most of the capital stack** if the ~$800M application is approved (Perpetua's $2.9B EXIM loan shows the program writes big checks for unbuilt U.S. mines).
- The **policy backdrop is the most favorable in modern history** (USGS designation, Section 232 scope including scandium/niobium/titanium, Project Vault, China scandium export controls).
- **Scandium could become strategically important** if defense/aerospace aluminum-scandium demand develops (hypersonics, lightweighting, additive manufacturing) — NioCorp is integrating downstream to help create that demand.
- A **binding offtake or price floor** (the MP Materials template: equity + $110/kg floor + offtake) would attack the demand problem and is the configuration that historically produced the largest, most durable rerates.

## Bear case

- **2022 economics are old**; the updated feasibility study (rare-earth circuits, new flowsheet, post-2019 costs) is **pending and could move materially** in either direction.
- **Scandium drives ~62% of project revenue**, but the **global market is tiny** (~30–40 t/yr) versus Elk Creek's planned ~100 t/yr — this is demand *creation*, not a supply-gap fill.
- **Disclosed offtakes don't absorb the planned scandium output** — only up to ~12 t/yr is under a binding (intermediary, not end-user) agreement; the rest is non-binding/best-efforts.
- **EXIM is not committed** — a non-binding LOI plus an application in technical/environmental review, pre-board, with the company's own "no assurances" language and no disclosed Plan-B lender.
- **Government attention ≠ financing** — the only enacted NB-specific cash is the $10M study grant (~0.9% of capex); Trump/Vance/Rubio have **not named NioCorp**.
- **Dilution could be severe** — filling an ~$800M gap with equity at a depressed price could roughly **triple the share count**, and the company already funds itself by serial issuance.
- **Comparable government-catalyst stocks often faded** — Lithium Americas fell ~33% within 60 days of its DOE deal; Perpetua's binding $2.9B EXIM approval produced no pop; NioCorp's own award lagged the mining sector within 180 days.
- **Competitors/incumbents may receive government demand first** — DLA's scandium stockpile dollars went **sole-source to Rio Tinto**, and DoD funded a competing domestic scandium project (ElementUS) and niobium supplier (Global Advanced Metals).

## What would make NB investable

NB becomes **more than a trade** only if **all** of the following hold:

- **EXIM or equivalent project debt becomes binding** (a board authorization / signed loan, not an LOI).
- **Scandium demand becomes binding** through a definitive offtake, a government stockpile purchase that names NioCorp, or a price floor.
- **The updated FS confirms survivable economics** at realistic costs and recoveries.
- **Dilution is known and acceptable** — the remaining equity need is sized and raised at non-punitive prices.
- **The construction schedule is funded** end-to-end, removing going-concern risk.

## What would make NB only tradeable

NB is **only a trade** (spike-and-fade risk) if it gets:

- A **political mention without money** (a name-check from an official with no committed dollars).
- **Sector policy** that helps critical minerals generally but does not name NioCorp.
- **Favorable but non-binding** government language (EOs, fact sheets, praise).
- **EXIM still in review** rather than committed.
- **DoD funding that stays small and research-focused** (more study grants, not procurement/offtake).

## Final investor read

NioCorp is **not mainly a geology bet anymore — it is a financing, scandium-demand, and government-commitment bet.** Political attention can move the stock, but the event history in this package shows those moves **often fade unless the catalyst includes binding money, binding demand, or a complete capital stack.** The deposit is real and the policy tailwind is real; what is not yet real is committed project debt, committed scandium demand, or an updated study confirming the economics. Until those convert from language into signed dollars, treat NB as **tradeable on catalysts and investable only on commitments** — and watch the EXIM decision and any scandium offtake/stockpile naming NioCorp as the events that decide which one it is.
