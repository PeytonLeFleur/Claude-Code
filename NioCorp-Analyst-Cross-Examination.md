# NioCorp (NB) — Analyst Cross-Examination (50 Answers)

*Blunt self-critique of the research package. Grounded in the repo plus four fresh verification agents run 2026-06-21 (EXIM mechanics, filings audit, scandium market, pre-revenue re-rate sample). Where a number is external/live rather than from the repo it is marked 🔶. Not investment advice.*

> **The single most important update from this round:** 🔶 **USGS MCS-2026 puts scandium oxide at ~$640/kg (2025 est.), down from ~$1,000 in 2021 — about one-sixth of the FS's $3,675/kg, with global production already ~80 t/yr (the package's "30–40 t/yr" understated supply).** This is the fact I'm least comfortable having under-weighted, and it cuts straight at the project's main value driver.

## A. Confidence & sourcing (Q1–6)

**1. Least-confident answers / most-damaging "disclosed" fact.** Least confident: the scandium revenue/price answers and the fully-diluted share count (a derived figure, not a single disclosed line). The single "disclosed" fact that most damages the thesis if wrong is the **$3,675/kg scandium price** — 61.7% of LOM revenue rides on it, and 🔶 the actual market is ~$640/kg. If the realized price is anywhere near spot, the repo's own sensitivity table drives NPV toward ~$261M or below.

**2. Did we ask NioCorp IR?** No. I have no ability to contact IR; everything here is public filings, releases, and third-party data. So every "not disclosed" means "not in the public record I could reach," not "IR declined." Treat the ~25 gaps as un-asked, not refused.

**3. Press releases vs. independent filings, and conflicts.** Roughly 60/40 leaning on NioCorp's own releases for *narrative* (offtakes, EXIM progress, scandium strategy), with SEC 10-K/10-Q/the 2022 TRS and USGS/EXIM/DoD primary docs anchoring the *hard numbers*. Conflicts found: the TRS itself is internally inconsistent (Nb $39.60 vs $46.56/kg; TiO₂ $0.88 vs $0.99; 32 vs 38-yr life; payback 2.69 vs 3.7y), and the press headline NPV (~$1.7B) differs from the TRS economic-model NPV ($2,350M). I used the TRS economic-model set and flagged it.

**4. NPDES discrepancy — resolved?** Yes. The redesign to "Zero Process Liquid Discharge" **eliminated the need** for an NPDES permit; NioCorp does **not** hold one (confirmed against the 2019 FS update carried into the technical report behind the FY2025 10-K, filed ~Sept 2025). Other framings that didn't fully survive: "100% of production under commercial agreements" depends on a **non-binding Traxys term sheet** ("would result in…"); "$800M EXIM" is an **LOI, not a commitment**; "construction launched" portal is **~$1.8M of $44.6M spent**.

**5. Freshest source / what could have moved.** Freshest: a **June 1, 2026 CEO op-ed**; the 10-Q filed **May 14, 2026**. The **updated FS is still not released** as of 2026-06-21. What could have moved since: an EXIM step, an equity raise, the FS itself, a new offtake, or insider Form 4s (I could not verify April–June 2026 Form 4s — EDGAR ownership pages blocked).

**6. Which Appendix-B pull most changes the view.** The **full updated Feasibility Study** when it lands — it resets capex, recoveries, REE economics, and (critically) the scandium price assumption the whole thesis hinges on. Second: the 10-Q XBRL for an exact fully-diluted count.

## B. EXIM & financing (Q7–13)

**7. Actual probability of full ~$800M board authorization.** ~**35%** (range 25–45%). For: EXIM's active critical-minerals posture, Perpetua/ioneer precedent, supportive chairman. Against: LOI outstanding **3+ years** with no board date, FS precondition unmet, discretionary export-nexus, the **same LOI given to an Australian competitor**, and an independent technical review that will stress a scandium bank-case far below $3,675/kg.

**8. Why would NB price differently than Perpetua's "no pop"?** It might pop *more in %* off a lower, less-de-risked base (Perpetua already had ~$80M+ DoD money and years of telegraphing). 🔶 Correction to the package: Perpetua actually rose **~+12%** on approval (CNBC), not zero — so "muted/pre-priced," not "no pop." But NB's EXIM angle is also 3+ years telegraphed, so a clean re-rate isn't safe to assume; durability hinges on equity-completion being resolved simultaneously.

**9. Does the Australian LOI weaken NB's headline?** Yes. An EXIM **Letter of Interest is a routine funnel instrument**, not selection — EXIM handed one to Sunrise/Syerston (~$67M), a direct scandium competitor. The $800M is an aspirational ceiling EXIM "may consider," marking NB as *in the queue*, not financed.

**10. If EXIM stalls past 2027 (vs. rejects)?** Death by dilution. With Yorkville expired and no Plan-B lender, NB funds G&A/engineering from **equity (ATM/offerings/converts)**, share count climbs, going-concern language risks appearing, and indicative terms/offtakes can lapse. A stall impairs the equity without EXIM ever saying "no."

**11. Does the domestic slate clear EXIM's export-nexus at all?** Only via the **discretionary critical-minerals "deemed-export" carve-out** (15% nexus satisfied by offtake into U.S.-manufactured goods that support exports), not a hard 15%-physically-exported test. This is real and underappreciated risk: it's exactly where EXIM has latitude to say no, and I could find **no public EXIM nexus determination specific to Elk Creek**.

**12. What in the SLR E&S review could block/delay — any sign of trouble?** No public sign of a problem yet. Realistic delay vectors: groundwater/dewatering (the FS carries ~$100M mine-water capex), tailings/paste-backfill, Category-A greenfield scrutiny. It's more a **timeline risk than a kill risk**, but it's a gating item with no disclosed category.

**13. If EXIM funds less than requested — dilution / break point.** Per the repo: $400M debt + $400M equity ≈ **221M shares**; $0 debt/$800M equity ≈ **296M** (≈**446M** at a stressed $2.66). The thesis breaks when the equity is raised below ~$3 — at the 446M-share path, even **full FS economics** yield only ~$5/sh, and any scandium haircut takes it to ~$2.

## C. Scandium demand & price (Q14–21)

**14. Concrete evidence demand is *appearing*.** A **definitive, priced, multi-year offtake with a real end-user** (not Traxys the trader); a DLA/NDS purchase **naming NB**; or Stellantis converting its proposal to binding volume. None of these exists today.

**15. Any path to the rest that isn't NB creating its own demand?** Thin. Credible non-self paths are SOFC/Bloom-type power, aerospace primes, and Stellantis auto — but all are unproven or non-binding. As of now it's one 12 t/yr trader floor + one $10M R&D program + one unsigned auto deal. The residual ~85–90 t/yr realistically **requires NB to build the market itself.**

**16. What price survives a 2.5–3× supply shock vs. $3,675/kg?** 🔶 Basic market logic says flooding a thin ~80 t/yr market craters price toward marginal/byproduct economics — i.e., toward the **current ~$640/kg**, not $3,675. The FS price embeds NB *simultaneously creating* a large high-value market; absent that, $3,675/kg is an aspiration. Price already fell ~35% (2021→2025) on far smaller additions.

**17. What would it take for the government to buy NB's scandium — any sign?** NB would need to be **producing qualified material**; DLA's "only vendor available" language means it buys who can deliver to spec *now* (Rio Tinto). No public sign of a DLA purchase naming NB, and stockpile volumes (single-digit tonnes) can't absorb 104 t/yr anyway.

**18. How long to build an Al-Sc end-market — is the lag priced?** **Decade-class.** Aerospace qualification runs 5–10+ yrs per platform; Al-Sc has been "about to scale" for ~20 years. The lag is **not** priced into a base case that assumes $3,675/kg × 104 t/yr from start-up.

**19. At what scandium price is the project *unfinanceable* (not just lower-NPV)?** Below roughly the FS after-tax breakeven (~$1,130/kg, a package estimate). But lenders underwrite a **bank-case** well below FS price — at 🔶 ~$640 spot with only trader offtake, a scandium-*dependent* project is **effectively unfinanceable** on conventional terms; NB's lender appeal leans on **niobium + government guarantees**, with scandium treated as upside.

**20. Built but scandium can't be sold at volume — then what?** Real scenario. NPV collapses toward the **Nb+Ti residual**; the equity is impaired even though the mine exists. This is the under-modeled tail: execution success + commercial failure.

**21. Aerospace "about to take off" for a decade — what's different, base rate?** Genuinely new: additive manufacturing (Scalmalloy-class), defense funding, supply-security urgency. But the **base rate for on-schedule arrival is poor**; industry writeups still frame 2026–2030 as the *prospective* phase. Bet on optionality, not a dated ramp.

## D. Feasibility, capex, technical (Q22–28)

**22. How much weight on a 4-year-old, zero-REE base case?** Low as a *point estimate*, useful as a *framework*. The $2,350M NPV is a 2019-cost, $3,675/kg-scandium artifact; treat it as a ceiling scenario, not a forecast, until the updated FS lands.

**23. Believe the "≤$1.2B" capex ceiling?** Skeptically. It's **management's figure, not independently validated**, and it asks you to believe ~$300M of optimizations offset 6 years of inflation *plus* new REE/TiCl₄ circuits on a 9.79%-contingency base. Hard-rock feasibility capex routinely overruns 20–40%.

**24. Are the ~$300M optimizations independently validated?** No — management's numbers (twin ramps −$188M, no acid plant −$100M). Plausible directionally; unverified until the updated FS and an independent technical review confirm them.

**25. First-of-kind flowsheet risk (esp. REE separation) — honestly?** Material. The full integrated flowsheet has run only at **demonstration/pilot scale**; commercial REE solvent-extraction separation is the newest, least-proven step. First-of-kind plants commonly miss early recovery/throughput/ramp targets — a real schedule-and-cost risk, not a rounding error.

**26. Is 40.3% titanium recovery optimistic, or is "doubling" real?** Titanium is ~2% of revenue, so it barely moves NPV either way. The "potential to double" is demo-scale and unproven at commercial scale; I wouldn't underwrite either the 40% or the double.

**27. Realistic all-in capex vs. the ±15% 2019 number?** Loading financing fees, interest-during-construction, working capital, inflation, and overrun risk onto a $1,141M ±15% 2019-dollar figure, a realistic all-in is plausibly **$1.3–1.6B+** — none of which is in the FS headline. The package flags this as not disclosed.

**28. If the updated FS lands worse — how far does it move you, probability?** Meaningfully bearish. P(worsens as published) ~35%, and even an "in-line" FS that keeps $3,675/kg may not be *believed* given spot. A worse FS widens the funding gap exactly when dilution hurts most.

## E. Capital structure & ownership (Q29–33)

**29. Central-case share count at first production?** Not the worst-case triple. Central: a **debt-heavy mix** if EXIM partially funds → ~**200–300M shares** by first production (vs. 145.6M now), with the 446M stress path reserved for an all-equity, low-price scenario.

**30. How fast does $419M deplete once full construction starts unfinanced?** Fast. Against a ~$1.1B build, $419M is **under five months of full-construction burn** if drawn down at project pace — which is precisely why construction can't truly start before financing closes; pre-financing, the cash stretches via slow portal/engineering spend.

**31. Is equity the only flexible lever left?** Essentially yes. Yorkville expired; offtake prepayments/royalties/streams are unquantified and unsigned. If debt slips, **equity (ATM/offerings/converts) is the lever**, which is the dilution engine.

**32. Does insider buying tell you anything?** Little. CEO open-market buys (~183k shares; a 46.8k warrant exercise) are **real skin but small** relative to a ~$770M cap and an ~$800M gap — sentiment signal, not a needle-mover, and not a substitute for the institutional capital the project needs.

**33. Citadel as the only 5%+ outside holder — signal?** **Not a conviction signal.** Citadel's 6.34% reads as market-making/index/multi-strat flow, not a strategic anchor. The absence of a strategic 5%+ holder (a major miner, an end-user, a sovereign fund) is itself telling.

## F. Event study & method (Q34–39)

**34. Is the sample big enough to be a rule?** No — it's a **directional prior, not a law**. n≈6–8, one 18-month regime, one theme, overlapping windows, no out-of-sample. The honest claim: "in this regime, pre-revenue gov-funded developers have not durably out-held the sector."

**35. How clean are the return figures?** Mixed. Core series (NB, MP, LAC, SPY, XME) re-fetched and cross-checked; but scraped tables had corrupted rows (some 2026 prints), and I marked those data-n/a rather than use them. Treat the windows as **good-to-2-significant-figures, not audited.**

**36. Any pre-revenue developer that durably re-rated on a gov catalyst?** **No clean one.** Durable holders (Centrus, MP, Lynas) all had revenue/profit; every pre-revenue twin (LAC, Oklo, Perpetua, Syrah, NB itself) faded from peak. That's the cleanest finding supporting caution.

**37. Is MP a bull template or a warning?** More **warning**. MP got the full trifecta (equity + $110/kg floor + offtake) and still gave back ~39% from its October peak and lost relative alpha to miners by ~180d — and MP had **revenue**; NB doesn't. MP shows a government package buys a *floor*, not perpetual momentum.

**38. Could the fade be the 2024–26 regime, not structure?** Partly — and I can't dismiss it. The critical-minerals rotation (REMX +>140%/yr) lifted everything and mean-reverted; that's a major confounder. But the **fundamental sort within the cohort** (revenue holds, pre-revenue fades) is a structural signal regime beta alone doesn't explain.

**39. Which catalyst is the market underpricing?** A **binding scandium offtake/price floor naming NB** (X3) — it attacks the harder problem (demand), isn't telegraphed like EXIM, and the analogs say it's the most durable catalyst type. The market is anchored on EXIM; demand is the bigger, less-priced swing.

## G. Position, conviction, and the honest read (Q40–50)

**40. Long, short, or flat?** I don't hold positions — I'm an AI, not a market participant. If forced to characterize where the *evidence* points: this is a **flat-to-small-speculative-option** profile, not a core long, until financing and scandium demand de-risk.

**41. Single event that flips trade → investment?** **Full financing close** (EXIM debt + equity + a binding, priced scandium offtake) — the conjunction that removes both financing and demand risk. Not EXIM alone; not a mention.

**42. Strongest argument against my own thesis I couldn't fully refute?** That **government strategic intent can override commodity economics** — if Washington decides domestic scandium/niobium is non-negotiable for defense, it could fund and offtake NB regardless of the $640/kg market, MP-style. I lean against it (the dollars went to Rio Tinto), but I can't rule it out, and it's the cleanest bull path.

**43. If wrong, more likely too bearish or too bullish, and on what?** Most likely **too bullish on scandium price** (I largely carried the FS $3,675/kg through the package before the $640 spot surfaced). Secondarily I could be **too bearish on EXIM** if the administration's critical-minerals push forces a fast yes.

**44. The fact most people are sleeping on?** 🔶 **Scandium spot is ~$640/kg — one-sixth of the FS assumption — and the market is already ~80 t/yr before NB adds a tonne.** The whole bull narrative quotes $3,675/kg; the tape says $640.

**45. What makes NB a zero, and how likely?** EXIM rejects or stalls indefinitely → forced dilution at collapsing prices → going-concern → recap/insolvency, with scandium economics too weak to attract a rescue. Not a literal $0 (the niobium deposit has salvage/strategic value), but an **80–95% equity wipeout path is a real ~25–35% probability** over the next few years.

**46. What makes NB a 5–10×, and how likely?** Full EXIM debt (minimal dilution) **+** a binding priced scandium offtake/floor **+** an FS that holds **+** construction on track → re-rate toward NAV at near-production multiples ($11–16+ on FS scandium). Requires the conjunction; I'd put it at **~10–15%**.

**47. Three data points to watch, and where.** (1) **EXIM board agenda / authorization** — exim.gov board-meeting pages + Federal Register + NB 8-Ks (SEC EDGAR CIK 0001512228). (2) **Updated FS + its scandium price assumption** — niocorp.com / 8-K. (3) **A binding, priced scandium offtake or DLA purchase naming NB** — NB press releases / SAM.gov / DLA notices.

**48. How to size a binary like this?** As an **option premium you can lose entirely** — small, position-sized for an ~80%+ drawdown, with the understanding that the payoff is fat-tailed and event-gated. Not a position to average down on through a stall.

**49. Lower-risk way to express the thesis?** Yes — a **revenue-generating, profitable critical-minerals name with government backing** (the cohort's durable holders, e.g., a producer with offtake) captures the policy tailwind without NB's binary financing/demand risk; or a basket/ETF (REMX-type) to own the theme without single-name wipeout risk. NB is the highest-beta way to express it.

**50. Six months out — most likely state, and the early break signal?** Most likely: **still pre-financing** — updated FS possibly out, EXIM still not at a board vote, more dilution, stock chopping in a **~$3.50–6.50** band on headlines. The early break signal (before the crowd): **an equity raise priced at a discount while EXIM is still "in review,"** and/or an **updated FS that quietly trims the scandium price or recovery** — either tells you the financing/economics are slipping before any formal EXIM "no."
