## 1. Executive Summary

**Bull case (one paragraph).** The policy backdrop for domestic critical minerals is the most favorable in modern history, and NioCorp sits inside it with two genuinely company-specific links: a **DoD DPA Title III award of up to $10M to subsidiary Elk Creek Resources Corp.** (Aug 5, 2025) — real federal cash plus strategic validation — and an **active EXIM "Make More in America" loan application of up to ~$800M** that has cleared Phase I (TRC-1), received a preliminary indicative term sheet, and advanced into independent technical (RPMGlobal) and environmental/social (SLR) review, with EXIM's own Chairman calling Elk Creek "exactly the type of opportunity EXIM was always designed to support." Scandium — NioCorp's largest value driver — is named in the DoD award, was added to China's export-control list (Apr 2025), appears in the Section 232 mineral scope, and is being stockpiled by DLA; niobium, scandium, and titanium are all on the USGS Critical Minerals List (niobium ranked #10 by supply-risk urgency). The MP Materials–DoD deal (equity + $110/kg NdPr price floor + offtake → +85% stock) and Perpetua's $2.9B EXIM loan prove the U.S. government will write large checks and even guarantee prices for domestic critical-mineral projects — a template that, if applied to NioCorp, would be transformational.

**Bear case (one paragraph).** Strip the press-release framing and the hard facts are thin: the **only enacted NioCorp-specific federal cash is the ~$10M study grant — under ~1% of project capex — and it funds engineering/drilling, not construction, procurement, offtake, or a price floor.** The Lockheed Martin scandium work is funded *inside* that same $10M, not on top of it. The headline **$800M EXIM figure is not a commitment** — it is a non-binding Letter of Interest plus an application still in due diligence, **pre-Board, with NioCorp's own filings warning financing may not come "on acceptable terms, or at all,"** and EXIM handed the identical LOI instrument to an Australian scandium competitor (Sunrise/Syerston, ~$67M). Most damning: **when the U.S. government actually spent procurement dollars on scandium (DLA, ~$40–45M), it went sole-source to Rio Tinto in Quebec and APL — not NioCorp** — and DoD separately funded a competing domestic scandium project (ElementUS, $29.9M) and a competing niobium supplier (Global Advanced Metals, DLA IDIQ up to $50M). **Trump, Vance, and Rubio have never named NioCorp.** There is **no binding government offtake and no scandium price floor.** Nebraska's LB1165 is conditional tax flexibility with no appropriation. The favorable policy is overwhelmingly **sector-wide tailwind that every competitor also receives.**

**Bottom line (one paragraph).** Government support has moved NioCorp from "ignored" to "endorsed counterpart," and that is not nothing — but it is **not yet load-bearing.** The single item that would make government support genuinely thesis-changing — an **EXIM Board final commitment** — has not occurred; everything else is a small study grant, sector-level policy, supportive home-state rhetoric, and a defense-demand signal that has so far paid competitors. Tellingly, NioCorp raised ~$500M privately in 2025–26 and started its $45M portal **without** the government money, which underscores that federal cash is not currently the deciding variable. **Net: government policy is a real and improving tailwind (IMPORTANT), but on today's evidence it does NOT by itself make NB investable, and it has NOT de-risked the two things that actually matter — closing the ~$800M+ financing gap and creating real scandium demand.** The thesis still rides on the EXIM Board vote and on converting non-binding offtake into binding tonnes.

## 8. Final Investor Conclusion

**Direct answers, blunt:**

1. **Has Trump personally named NioCorp?** **NO — NO EVIDENCE FOUND.** All apparent links are NioCorp's framing of generic policy.
2. **Has Vance personally named NioCorp?** **NO — NO EVIDENCE FOUND.** He named *scandium* generically; not NioCorp.
3. **Has Rubio personally named NioCorp?** **NO — NO EVIDENCE FOUND.**
4. **Has any U.S. government agency directly funded NioCorp or its subsidiary?** **YES — once:** DoD DPA Title III, **up to $10M** to Elk Creek Resources Corp. (Aug 5, 2025), milestone-gated, for engineering/drilling/cost work (~0.9% of capex). That is the only enacted NioCorp-specific federal cash.
5. **Is EXIM financing committed?** **NO.** It is an application in technical/environmental due diligence (TRC-2), pre-Board, non-binding. Commitment level ≈ 3–4/10. **NO EVIDENCE FOUND** of any Board authorization.
6. **Is government policy favorable?** **YES, at the sector level** — strongly so (USGS designation, EO 14241, §232 scope incl. scandium/niobium/titanium, Project Vault, China scandium controls, MP/Perpetua precedents). But it is **POLICY TAILWIND shared with competitors**, not DIRECT NIOCORP support, and the China truce + competitor awards cut the other way.
7. **Is government support enough to make NB investable by itself?** **NO.** On today's evidence, federal support de-risks — at most — a feasibility study and an alloy-qualification program. It has **not** closed the ~$800M+ financing gap, **not** created binding demand, and **not** set a scandium price floor. NioCorp's own ~$500M private raise (funding *around* the government) confirms federal cash is not currently the deciding variable. Government policy is a *reason to watch*, not a *reason to own by itself*.
8. **Single government event that would most change the stock thesis:** an **EXIM Board final commitment** authorizing the ~$800M loan (turns "interest" into committed, largely non-dilutive construction capital). A close second: a **government scandium price floor / offtake** (MP-style), which would de-risk *demand* — the harder of NioCorp's two problems. Neither has occurred.
9. **Single missing government commitment that is the biggest risk:** the **absence of any binding government offtake or scandium price floor**, compounded by **no committed EXIM debt and no disclosed Plan-B lender.** If EXIM rejects/under-funds, there is no government backstop — and the only real scandium procurement dollars to date went to competitors.

**Overall classification of "U.S. government support" for NioCorp today:** **POLICY TAILWIND + one small DIRECT ELK CREEK grant + a pending (not committed) FINANCING TAILWIND.** Confidence HIGH. Materiality **IMPORTANT but not yet THESIS CHANGING.** The thesis becomes THESIS CHANGING the day EXIM's Board authorizes the loan or a scandium price floor/offtake is set — and not before.
