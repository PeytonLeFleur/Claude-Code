# NioCorp Investor Decision Memo

*A one-page synthesis of the [NioCorp Elk Creek Investor Diligence Package](./README.md). Inputs: the four package documents only. As of 2026-06-21. Not investment advice; no price target.*

## Current thesis

NioCorp (NASDAQ: NB) is a **high-risk, development-stage critical-minerals stock**. The core bet is **no longer mainly geology** — the Elk Creek deposit is defined and the products (niobium, scandium, titanium, potential rare earths) sit on the federal critical-minerals list. The core bet is now **financing, scandium demand, government commitment, dilution, and execution**: closing a ~$1.1B capital stack (anchored by an in-review EXIM debt application of up to ~$800M), creating real demand for scandium (which drives ~62% of project revenue into a global market of only ~30–40 t/yr), converting favorable government *language* into *binding* dollars, and surviving the equity dilution required to get there. Get those right and the published economics imply large upside; miss on financing or scandium demand and the equity faces severe dilution or worse.

## Decision box

| Action | When it makes sense | What must be true |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy now** | Only as a **speculative, catalyst-driven** position sized for total loss | The investor explicitly **accepts** financing risk (EXIM not committed), scandium-demand risk (only ~12 t/yr under binding offtake), dilution risk (an ~$800M raise could ~triple the share count), and **binary government-catalyst risk** — and is buying the *option* on a catalyst, not the current fundamentals |
| **Wait** | When you want the binary resolved before committing capital | You are waiting for a **named, binding** event: an **EXIM final commitment**, a **binding scandium offtake**, a **DLA stockpile purchase naming NioCorp**, the **updated feasibility study**, or a **full capital-stack close** — any of which converts the thesis from language to signed money/demand |
| **Avoid** | When the risk set is outside tolerance | The investor **cannot tolerate** dilution, financing failure (no disclosed Plan-B lender), weak/undeveloped scandium demand, or **catalyst-fade risk** — the event study shows comparable government-catalyst moves often fade |

## Most important variables (ranked)

1. **EXIM final commitment or equivalent project debt** — *Status:* application in technical + environmental/social review, **pre-board, not committed**; no EXIM board agenda item or Federal Register notice naming NioCorp found. *Why it matters:* anchors ~65% of remaining capital with non-dilutive debt; no disclosed Plan-B lender. *Evidence that changes the decision:* an EXIM **board authorization** (not an LOI).
2. **Binding scandium demand (offtake, stockpile, or price floor)** — *Status:* only **up to ~12 t/yr** under a binding (intermediary, not end-user) agreement; the rest is non-binding/best-efforts; ~54% of project value is **uncommitted scandium**. *Why it matters:* attacks the harder of the two core problems — demand creation in a tiny market. *Evidence:* a **definitive, priced, multi-year** offtake or a government stockpile/price floor naming NioCorp.
3. **Updated feasibility study** — *Status:* **pending**, not yet released; 2022 economics are the live numbers. *Why it matters:* resets the base case (capex, recoveries, rare-earth circuits, post-2019 costs) in either direction. *Evidence:* the published updated NI 43-101 / S-K 1300, with capex at/below ~$1.1–1.2B.
4. **Full capital stack** — *Status:* **not closed**; ~$1.1B gap; ~$500M raised 2025–26. *Why it matters:* removes going-concern risk and is the only configuration that historically produced a durable rerate. *Evidence:* EXIM + equity + offtake + strategic capital all signed.
5. **Dilution terms** — *Status:* company funds itself by **serial equity issuance**; ~145.6M shares; worst case modeled near a **tripling** of the share count. *Why it matters:* the size/price of the remaining raise determines per-share outcome. *Evidence:* offering/ATM filings sizing the raise at non-punitive prices.
6. **Government support becoming binding** — *Status:* only enacted NB-specific cash is a **$10M study grant (~0.9% of capex)**; Trump/Vance/Rubio have **not named NioCorp**; scandium stockpile dollars went to **Rio Tinto**. *Why it matters:* policy tailwind is shared with competitors until dollars name NioCorp. *Evidence:* a contract/award/offtake naming NioCorp.
7. **Construction execution** — *Status:* $44.6M portal underway; full construction **contingent on financing**. *Why it matters:* budget/schedule overrun widens the gap and dilution. *Evidence:* on-budget portal completion and a funded full-construction start.

## What would make the stock more than a trade

**NB becomes investable only when signed money and signed demand replace policy language.** Required signs:

- **Binding EXIM or equivalent project debt** (board authorization, not an LOI)
- **Binding scandium demand** (definitive offtake, stockpile purchase, or price floor naming NioCorp)
- **Updated FS confirms** survivable economics at realistic costs and recoveries
- **Dilution is known** — the remaining equity need is sized and priced
- **Construction financing is complete** end-to-end

## What would make it only a trade

**NB is only tradeable if the catalyst is political, non-binding, small, research-oriented, or sector-wide.** Examples:

- A **Trump mention without money**
- **Critical-minerals policy not naming NioCorp**
- A **small DoD research grant**
- **EXIM still in review**
- **Non-binding offtake language** (term sheets, MOUs)

## Kill switch checklist

- [ ] EXIM rejected
- [ ] EXIM delayed without explanation
- [ ] Updated FS worsens economics
- [ ] Capex materially increases
- [ ] Scandium market fails to absorb planned output
- [ ] Government buys from Rio Tinto or another competitor instead
- [ ] Equity raise is heavily dilutive
- [ ] Binding offtakes do not appear
- [ ] Construction delay after financing
- [ ] Metallurgy or recovery problem

## Final read

NB has **asymmetric upside only if government language converts into binding financing and binding demand.** Without that, the stock can spike on headlines, but the research package shows that **many government-catalyst moves fade** — structural twins (Lithium Americas, MP Materials, Perpetua, NioCorp's own award) popped and then gave back their gains, while the only durable rerates belonged to already-profitable companies. **EXIM and scandium demand matter more than a political mention.** Treat NB as tradeable on catalysts and investable only on commitments.
